      About the images

      When does a painting enter the public domain?

      In the EU, Poland included, copyright lasts for the artist’s life plus 70 years, counted from the 1 January after death. Klee (died 1940) has been free since 2011, Matisse (1954) since 2025, Tanguy (1955) since January 2026, Rodchenko (1956) becomes free on 1 January 2027 and Picasso (1973) in 2044. The United States counts from publication: works published before 1931 are public domain there now, and every 1 January another year’s worth joins them.

      Can a scan or photograph of an old painting be copyrighted?

      For a faithful reproduction of a flat public-domain work, generally no. Article 14 of the EU’s 2019 Copyright in the Digital Single Market Directive says such reproductions attract no copyright or related right unless the reproduction is itself an original creation, and member states, Poland among them, have written that into national law. In the US the same result comes from the 1999 case Bridgeman v. Corel. Three caveats: a photograph of a sculpture or other 3D object involves choices of angle and light, so the photographer can hold a copyright; museums can still impose conditions through visitor rules and website terms, which are contracts rather than copyright; and Italy’s cultural heritage code lets the state charge for commercial reproductions of works in its collections.

      How do scans end up on Wikimedia Commons?

      Mostly in bulk. Museums with open-access programmes, such as the Met, the Art Institute of Chicago, Yale, the National Gallery of Art and Paris Musées, release their images of public-domain works under CC0, and volunteers or bots copy them over; that is why some files here carry accession numbers in their names. In 2005 the Yorck Project’s DVD of 10,000 paintings was uploaded, which explains file names like “Paul Gauguin 091.jpg”. Volunteers photograph works on gallery walls (the Italian-titled files here come from one prolific photographer). And every Public Domain Day, when another year of artists comes free, brings a wave of new uploads.

      Are Google Arts & Culture scans public domain?

      The scans themselves gain no new copyright under the rules above, which is why Commons hosts thousands of “Google Art Project” files from a 2012 bulk upload of Google’s zoomable images. Two things still apply. Google’s terms of service govern how you use Google’s website, and the painting’s own copyright stays with the painting: a Google scan of a Picasso is still a Picasso.

      Why some frames show no picture

      Commons hosts a file only if the work is free both in the United States and in its country of origin. Duchamp’s 1912 Nude Descending a Staircase is public domain in the US but in copyright in France until 2039, so it isn’t there. Those cards link to the work’s Wikipedia article or the museum’s page instead, and each card shows the year the work becomes free in the EU.
